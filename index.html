How to Stay Safe

  • Wear SCBA to avoid toxic gases.
  • Use full turnout gear.
  • Use medical gloves and face shields.
  • Decontaminate gear after exposure.
  • Follow WHMIS training.
  • Limit time in smoke-heavy areas.

Materials Used in Firefighter Gear

Firefighter turnout gear is built from advanced heat‑resistant materials such as Nomex and Kevlar, providing flame resistance and durability. A moisture barrier—often made from Gore‑Tex—protects against steam burns and keeps firefighters dry. The thermal liner insulates against extreme heat, while reflective striping ensures visibility. Reinforced stitching and layered construction allow the gear to withstand harsh conditions and heavy use.

Firefighter Tools & Equipment

  • Halligan Bar: Forcible entry tool used for prying, breaching, and striking.
  • Flathead Axe: Used with the Halligan for chopping and ventilation.
  • Thermal Imaging Camera (TIC): Detects heat signatures to find victims and hidden fire.
  • Hose Lines: Deliver water from engines to the fire.
  • Nozzles: Control water flow patterns (straight stream, fog, etc.).
  • Ventilation Fans: Remove smoke and heat from buildings.
  • Rescue Struts: Stabilize vehicles during extrications.
  • K12 & Chainsaws: Cut through roofs, walls, and debris.
  • Radios: Critical communication tools for fireground coordination.
  • Medical Bags: Contain trauma supplies for EMS response.

Hazards & Protection

Firefighters face dangerous chemicals, heat, smoke, and toxic environments. Proper equipment and safety systems reduce exposure and injury risk.

Safety Equipment

  • Helmet
  • Turnout/Bunker Gear
  • Gloves
  • Boots
  • SCBA
  • Eye Protection

Other Safety Systems

  • Eye-wash stations
  • Emergency showers
  • Medical monitoring
  • Rehab areas
  • Decontamination lines
  • Communication systems

About the Fire Department

Fire departments serve as the frontline protectors of communities, responding to fires, medical emergencies, hazardous material incidents, rescues, and disasters. Firefighters train extensively to master equipment, tactics, and teamwork under pressure. Beyond emergency response, they provide public education, conduct safety inspections, enforce fire codes, and work proactively to reduce risks. Their mission is rooted in service, courage, and dedication to safeguarding lives and property.

Types of Firetrucks & Their Purposes

  • Tower Ladder: Aerial ladder with bucket platform for rescues and elevated water streams.
  • Rosenbauer T-Rex: Articulating boom for high‑reach rescues.
  • Pumper: Main water delivery unit with 500–1,000 gallon tank.
  • Engine: First‑due firefighting truck carrying hose, tools, and water.
  • Tanker: Transports 1,500–3,000+ gallons of water to rural areas.
  • Brush Truck: Off‑road unit for wildland fires.
  • Squad Truck: Multi‑role unit carrying medical and rescue gear.
  • Command Car: Used by chiefs to manage incidents.
  • Heavy Rescue: Carries extrication and stabilization tools.
  • Light Rescue: Smaller rescue unit for minor extrications.
  • Support Truck: Provides air bottles, lighting, rehab supplies.
  • Rosenbauer Viper: Advanced aerial apparatus with powerful boom.
